1 · Vision

A village where Muslim families live together, look after one another, grow their own food, make their own power and water, and keep their children learning and healthy, all in a way that does not depend on the city grid.

  • Together: families, masjid, madrasah and clinic within a short walk.
  • Self-sustaining: food, water, energy and village income stay in the village.
  • Off-grid: no mains power, water or sewer needed.
  • Step by step: start small with 10 families, then grow.

2 · Focus 1: Self-Sustainable Village

The community itself. Tap a line to open it.

Homes

Simple, comfortable homes for different family types: a small home (couple or elder), a family home and a large family home. Each has its own garden space. Rough sizes are in section 3.

Masjid

The heart of the village: prayer, Jumu'a, gatherings. Placed in the middle, easy to walk to from every home.

Madrasah

A classroom building for children and adults. The lessons come from MSA (section 5).

Clinic

A small clinic room, first aid and care space (section 4).

Community hall and shop

A hall for meetings, weddings and classes, and a small shop or market for village produce and goods.

Food

Family gardens, a shared orchard and vegetable plots, and a few animals (chickens, goats) as land and rules allow.

Water

Rain tanks first, a bore if the land allows, and filtering for drinking water. Details in section 3.

How the village is run

A shura (committee) chosen by the families, simple village rules written down together, and a clear way for a family to become a member.

Money

How a family joins and pays, shared running costs (power, water, upkeep), and village income (produce, workshops). To be worked out after the owner decides the questions in section 7.

3 · Focus 2: Off-Grid Living

How the village lives without the grid. The sizes below are rough ranges per family (about 5 people), only to start the plan. They will change once the place and climate are known.

PartRough size per family
Energy: solar panels4 to 8 kW
Energy: batteries10 to 20 kWh
Water: rain tank storage20,000 to 50,000 litres
Food growing: garden200 to 500 m²
Home (building)90 to 160 m²
Land per family (home, garden, share of village)0.2 to 0.5 hectare (to be worked out)

Rough planning ranges, not engineering advice. A qualified designer checks every figure before anything is built.

Energy

Solar panels, battery storage and a small backup (for example a generator for long cloudy spells). A shared village power point for the masjid, clinic and hall.

Water

Rain tanks on every roof, a bore if possible, simple filtering and treatment for drinking water, and a shared reserve tank for emergencies.

Waste and toilets

Composting toilets, grey water re-used on gardens, food scraps to compost, and a plan for what cannot be reused.

Food growing

Raised beds, a greenhouse, drip irrigation, and an orchard planted early because trees take years.

Building methods

Low-cost, cool in summer and warm in winter: good insulation, shade, and local materials such as rammed earth or timber where suitable.

4 · Health most important

Health is the most important part of the village. The village health plan is built around the MSA Health app. SSEVM only links to it; nothing is copied.

  • Clinic room with first-aid supplies and a visiting nurse or doctor.
  • Healthy food and living: gardens, clean water, exercise, good habits.
  • Care for mothers, children and elders.
  • Emergency plan: how to reach hospital care when far from town.

Anything the village needs added to the app is asked of the MSA Health team.

Open the MSA app (Health is inside it)

5 · Education

The whole of MSA (Madrasah Study App) is the village's madrasah learning. This plan only provides the madrasah building, class times and what the teachers need. The lessons stay in MSA.

Open the MSA app

6 · Phases

Phase 0 · Plan

Concept plan, choose the place, work out land size, site drawing, rough costs.

Phase 1 · First 10 families

Basic power and water, first homes, masjid and a simple classroom, gardens started, a clinic room.

Phase 2 · Grow

More families, the community hall and shop, orchard bearing fruit, shared village income started.

Phase 3 · Full village

All homes and shared buildings complete, the village mostly self-sufficient and run by its own shura.
